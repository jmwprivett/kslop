## Cross-quest observations

1. **The two quests are one dependency chain, not two projects.** Physical kcall ≈ PAC-correct control flow ≈ what the PAC quest is hunting. The `cs_allow_invalid` candidate is simultaneously the best first *use* of the kcall and the best *proof target* for the PAC primitive. Every doc already says this implicitly; the work plan should say it explicitly and stop ranking them as separate escalations.
2. **Your pattern is: close a route with publication-grade rigor, then stop running the cheap physical experiment.** Identity canary, cross-task canary, ATTACHEXC probe — all built, all fail-closed, all unexecuted. The marginal information per device-minute is currently highest there, not in more REA.
3. **The evidence discipline is genuinely rare.** VM-claimed vs physical-claimed boundaries, per-boot permission tickets, durable journals with `dispatchReturned:false` pre-armed checkpoints, negative results retained without overclaiming (the 1100 fileport, the trust-cache policy stop), and self-corrections (per-process scope, `0x7481` discriminator, `0x1b0` vs `0x1c0`) recorded in place. Keep this; it is what makes the "not tried" list trustworthy.
4. **The `.34` result is quietly the most important recent finding.** The aperture ignoring maxProt `r--` on `.34 dyldreadonly` means the read-only-data assumption across the whole shared cache is unreliable, in your favor. It also means data-page targets need per-boot identical-bytes tickets forever — which you already built.
5. **The KRW lease is a prerequisite for nearly everything downstream** (portability, SpringBoard-resident supervisor, kcall consumers) and requires zero exploitation. Its absence is a scheduling decision, not a research blocker.
